### AI Future vs AI Now

Many doomer AI predictions are hyperbolic, unsubstantiated, and ill-informed. These prognostications get all the attention while we ignore the many subtle ways that AI is changing society around us, which brings us to today's readings.

### Education

Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) stood up an ad hoc committee to investigate how its students, professors, and researchers were using AI. Last month, the committee published its findings. Bottom line is that "the advent of AI demands a broader, holistic reassessment of the nature, scope and purpose of higher education today."

AI adoption in the past four years (since ChatGpt 2022) has taken off at a rate faster than the internet, television, or telephone. AI is "changing what students need to know." The entire MIT curriculum "will likely need to be reexamined and potentially revamped."

They found that AI, like social media, is increasing isolation. "[T]he reliance on chatbots in education displaces peer-to-peer connections like study groups and decreases interactions between students and instructors." For this reason, the report recommends that instructors "include a regular in-person social component," something more than a lecture. 

The report recommended instructors start by reevaluating the goals of the courses they teach. The institutional response must be more than AI-proofing final exams. Professors need to figure out which concepts or skills they want to impart to their students. 

Ultimately, learning is more than the transfer of technology knowledge. "Education is a cultural practice through which students learn to make meaning, exercise judgment, form identities, and participate responsibly in community." This sounds hokey, but I do think it's broadly correct. As AI replaces or supersedes many human cognitive skills, it's worth thinking about what makes humans unique and focusing on that.  

### Mathematics

This exisential question flows into the [blog post "After Math" by Silvia De Toffoli and Eamon Duede](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/09/12/after-math/). The announcement that OpenAI solved a super hard math problem (Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem), engendered much of the same discussion about what actually is mathematics and what do mathematicians do?  

De Toffoli and Duede emphatically that there is far more to mathematics than solving problems. Unlike Go or chess, mathematics is not a game, and mathematicians are not trying to win, but to understand. Quoting Jeremy Avigad, "It is misguided to think of mathematicians as competing with AI; when we drive a car, we aren't competing to see who can go faster, and when we use a phone, we aren't competing to see who can speak louder."

AI is a tool, and it is useful to the extent that it can aid mathematical inquiry. Yes, OpenAI solved a challenging problem, but it failed to aid the study of mathematics because it failed to provide a proof intelligible to humans. 

Mathematics is a discipline far more expansive than solving problems; its goals include "developing new theories and techniques, understanding the world, sustaining a community, training the next generation of mathematicians, contributing to cumulative knowledge, and creating works of aesthetic value" (Terence Tao).

### Business

Folks from MIT NANDA (Challapally, Pease, Raskar, and Chari) published State of AI in Business 2025. I looked on their website for a 2026 version, but no luck. In looking for a recent version, I stubmled on criticism of the report's methodology and lack of data transparency. So I will skip reviewing that and instead pull in quotes from newsletters that I trust.  

[In his July 28 edition of Artificial Confidence](https://artificialconfidence.com/p/reading-the-tea-leaves-2026-predictions), Corey Quinn predicted that commerical software will continue to thrive because "while vibe coding is fun, vibe maintaining sucks, and vibe maintaining someone else’s slop is just the absolute worst.""

More broadly, "[t]he shape of some jobs may change; the tools we use as technologists certainly will. But the two things that can’t be industrialized are wisdom and judgement, and it’s becoming increasingly clear that LLMs aren’t on a path to changing that."

Again, the central theme of AI hot takes I agree with is the perspective that AI is a tool. 

### Software Engineering and AI

It will become very clear that I am subscriber and huge fan of the Pragmatic Engineer. Orosz covers the software engineering (SE) industry better than anyone else. He is especially atuned to the trends shaping SE. Because software engineering is a linguistic profession with minimal physical interaction, large language models will have early and profound influence on its future. Not a few industry leaders have predicted that AI will eliminate all software engineering. 

In the [April 14th edition of the Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-impact-of-ai-on-software-engineers-2026), Gergely Orosz and Elin Nlsson surveyed software engineers on how they use AI at work. They found that "companies seem to be in the experimentation phase with AI tools, and several respondents say that they believe their companies have unsustainable AI-tooling budgets."

[Gergely interviewed Dex Horthy,](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/context-engineering-with-dex-horthy) who "believes that the reason we see LLMs 'degrade' existing codebases is because they are optimized to do well on SWE-bench-style benchmarks. These benchmarks reward reproducing a known fix in codebases like Django, but cannot measure poor architecture decisions."

[Charity Majors articulated an analogy that I think about a lot](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/stop-being-skeptical-about-ai-for) but is rarely mentioned: AI is as transformative as cloud computing. She also draws an analogy of code evolving from "pets" to "cattle." Another way of phrasing that is code is advancing from bespoke tailoring to mass production, and engineers need to adapt their mindset of shipping code that they have not read all of.  

(Charity also gives career advice that aligns with my philosophy: run towards the waves.  In this instant, that means getting AI on your resume as fast as possible. I am a big believer in first-mover job switching to long-term tech trends such as software engineering, cloud computing, and now AI.)

The "[Inside Anthropic" edition of the newsletter](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/inside-anthropic) is the fortune teller because these are engineers at the bleeding edge of AI capability. How they use AI seems like a pattern we'll see industry wide. Engineers are now orchestrating the work of AI agents. Good engineers still need to understand their code, their infrastructure, and how it all coalesces together. To do this, they need to keep learning constantly. 

Most tellingly is the observation that, "the more hands-on someone is in software engineering, the less they believe the models will take their jobs." Just as in education and mathematics, there is much more to software engineering than what AI can do.
