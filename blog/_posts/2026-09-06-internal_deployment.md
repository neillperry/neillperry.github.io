### Governance of Internal Deployments

Apollo Research published a [report on the governance of internal deployments of frontier AI models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12170). The report addresses possible risks of the internal deployment of advanced AI models within AI labs. 


### Critique of the Report

As part of an application to a position at Apollo Research, I was asked to write a critique of the report.  What follows is my response: 

The research is insufficiently supported and logically flawed.  
First, the research assumes too many premises. There are too many enumerate in this critique, but they are mostly statements that include the words “likely,” “may,” or “could.” Some examples include: “there will likely be outsized economic benefits,” AI systems “may … have read, write, and execute permissions,” and “internal AI systems could theoretically be operated with fewer safety constraints.” The authors do not elaborate on why they believe these premises are likely nor do they cite any authority to that effect. So much conjecture leaves the reader with a hypothetical scenario whose probability they are unable to ascertain. It undermines the proposed solutions and makes the issue less urgent. 

Next, the report appeals to authority. The report accepts uncritically the predictions and observations from industry leaders with no attempt to ascertain their plausibility. The authors do not consider that those statements may be self-serving hype. For example, the report notes that in 2024 Marc Benioff stated that Salesforce will no longer hire software engineers in 2025 due to AI’s increased productivity. However, a search for “software engineer” on the Salesforce job website (https://www.salesforce.com/company/careers/jobs) returns over 500 results. If AI productivity increased as Benioff predicted, then why is Salesforce still hiring software engineers in 2026? The report should have either omitted Benioff’s prediction or pushed back on its veracity. 

There is a similar credulity with the prediction that AI will replace AI researchers themselves. The authors posit this with no explanation on all the tasks AI researchers perform and how AI will replace all of those. The report does not include interviews with AI researchers about their jobs, the skillsets they need, and whether the researchers believe AI will replace them. In other fields, there is literature on how AI is changing, but not replacing, researchers, but the report does not cite those articles or explain how the role of AI researcher differs from other disciplines (citations available upon request). 

Overall, the report relies too much on conjecture to provide useful guidance. Although the problem is possible, the report does no marshal sufficient evidence to establish its probability. When speaking about federal regulation of nuclear power, President Eisenhower observed, “It would be unwise to try to anticipate by law all of the many problems that are certain to arise.” The report makes this mistake here.
